Johannes Brinz

I work at the intersection of philosophy of computation, philosophy of neuroscience, and AI. My research focuses on neuromorphic computation and the question of whether artificial systems can duplicate—not merely simulate—the brain.

About me

Background & motivation

I am a doctoral researcher at the Institute of Philosophy, Osnabrück University, where I have been a Research Assistant since October 2022. My dissertation investigates the philosophical foundations of neuromorphic computation—asking whether brain-inspired hardware can genuinely duplicate the computational structure of biological neural systems, or whether it only simulates them.

My background is interdisciplinary: I hold a MA in Philosophy (TU Dresden, grade 1.1), a BSc in Physics (TU Dresden), and a BA in Philosophy (TU Dresden). Earlier, I studied Mechanical Engineering at TU Munich and have done exchange semesters in Lisbon and Belo Horizonte. This breadth informs my approach to the philosophy of mind and AI.

I am currently seeking postdoctoral positions in philosophy of mind, philosophy of AI, or philosophy of science, ideally starting in 2026/27.

Research interests

Topics I think and write about

My work centres on non-classical computation—specifically whether neuromorphic computers can duplicate the mammalian brain’s computational structure rather than merely approximate it. I draw on philosophy of science and philosophy of mind to analyse what kinds of models neurocomputational science produces and what it would mean for a machine to be computationally equivalent to a brain.

I am also an associated member of the Research Training Group Situated Cognition (Universities of Osnabrück and Bochum) and co-editor (with Gualtiero Piccinini) of a topical collection on Philosophy of Neuromorphic AI in Philosophy of AI.
